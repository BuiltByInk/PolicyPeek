# PolicyPeek UI audit, 2026-09-18

Scope: `frontend/` (Astro static site). Brief: make the UI work really well, find bugs and quirks, research best practice for "verdict / label / rating" products, avoid generic SaaS looks.

Evidence base: a real headless Chromium 151 (Playwright cache at `~/.cache/ms-playwright`, driven by `playwright-core`) against a local `astro preview`. Screenshots at 375, 768 and 1280 px in light and dark for home, /tools/, /about/ and one tool page per verdict (plus Zoom and Amazon Q edge cases). Screenshots are in `research/ui_audit_assets/shots/` (`before-*` and `after-*`). Everything marked "visual" was seen in a screenshot or measured in the browser. Everything marked "static" was read from source only.

Safety: every request to `api.getpolicypeek.com` was intercepted in the browser and answered by a mock. No request reached the live API (the interception log shows 0 non-local requests let through). `tools.json`, backend, D1 and secrets were not touched. Nothing was deployed.

Backup of the pre-audit source: `backups/frontend-src-pre-ui-audit-2026-09-18/`.

## 1. Research

### 1.1 What the good "verdict / label" products actually do

| Source | Transferable idea | Applied here? |
|---|---|---|
| FDA Nutrition Facts redesign ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/changes-nutrition-facts-label)) | One number is the loudest thing on the label: Calories and serving size were made larger and bold (calories no smaller than 22 pt). Hierarchy is by size, not by decoration. | Proposed (Option A): make the verdict the biggest type on a tool page. Today it is a small badge next to the name. |
| EU energy label rescale 2021 ([EU Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/focus-improved-eu-energy-label-paving-way-more-innovative-and-energy-efficient-products-2021-02-16_en), [ScienceDirect study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301421521003098)) | A scale is only useful if the categories still separate products. They dropped A+++ because most products bunched at the top and consumers misread "A+" as the best. | Supports keeping verdicts as distinct categories with different visual forms (already done), and against adding more shades. |
| Apple Privacy Nutrition Labels ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/privacy/labels/), [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/20/12/15/what-apples-new-privacy-nutrition-labels-say-about-some-of-the-biggest-apps)) | Plain sentences ("The developer does not collect any data from this app"), fixed category names, and an explicit state for "nothing provided" ("No Details Provided"). | Plain-sentence verdict meanings added (see 3.5). "We don't know" is an explicit state, not a mild grade: Option A hatches it. |
| ToS;DR ([tosdr.org](https://tosdr.org/en), [classification](https://tosdr.github.io/tosdr.org/classification.html)) | Letter grade A to E plus a separate **"No Grade Yet"** state for not enough data, and a visible methodology line ("transparent and peer-reviewed"). | Confirms `unrated` and `not_ai_tool` should sit outside the graded scale. The site already does this. |
| UK Food Hygiene Rating Scheme ([FSA display guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fhrs-displaying-your-rating)) | Separate official stickers for "Awaiting inspection", "Awaiting publication" and "Exempt": the not-yet-known and not-applicable cases are designed objects, not blanks. | Same idea as `unrated`, `shared_domain_unverified`, `not_ai_tool`. I could not read the sticker specs themselves (the guidance page returned little), so I am not claiming anything about their exact look. |
| Mozilla *Privacy Not Included ([about](https://foundation.mofostaging.net/en/privacynotincluded/about/), [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/mozilla-privacy-survey-finds-mental-health-and-prayer-apps-fail-privacy-test-pretty-spectacularly/)) | A **warning label** that is automatic when a minimum bar cannot be confirmed, three thumbs states (up / sideways / down), and the reason for the label is shown. | Reason-always-visible rule (charter) matches this. I could not load their live pages (HTTP 403), so I have search-snippet evidence only, not a look at their UI. |
| GOV.UK Design System, tags ([Tag](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/tag/)) | Don't rely on colour alone; keep colour meaning consistent across places; their tags moved from white-on-dark to light background with dark text because users mistook them for buttons; they dropped uppercase because it is harder to read for longer text. | Badges already carry text and distinct border styles, so colour is never the only signal. **Open point:** our badges are uppercase, including a 40-character one ("Company-wide policy — not product-verified"). See proposal P3. |
| GOV.UK error messages ([Error message](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-message/)) | Say what went wrong and how to fix it; avoid "invalid", "please", "sorry", "oops"; keep what the user typed; link the message to the field (`aria-describedby`), put focus on the problem. | Applied to SubmitTool (see 3.4). |
| Data journalism (ProPublica, FT, Bloomberg via [ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/nerds/design-principles-for-news-apps-graphics), [GIJN on FT](https://gijn.org/stories/data-visualization-storytelling-tips-john-burn-murdoch/)) | Direct labelling; an "annotation layer" that puts words next to the thing; every chart names its source, date and caveats; serif for headlines at the Guardian. | Applied: home page verdict register is a labelled table with counts instead of feature cards. Proposed (Option B): tool's own words as an attributed pull-quote. |
| Consumer Reports labels ([CR](https://www.consumerreports.org/media-room/press-releases/2026/02/consumer-reports-labels-explained-recommended-top-pick-best-brand-best-of-and-smart-buy)) | Small fixed vocabulary of named designations. | Consistent with our six named verdicts. I could not confirm how they show "not tested"; not claiming it. |
| Dark-mode status colours ([Material dark theme](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html), summaries) | Use desaturated tints in dark mode; saturated colours vibrate and fail contrast; warning yellow is usually redesigned as amber. | Already how the dark tokens are built. Measured: all pass (see 2.2). |
| Wirecutter ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirecutter_(website)) and search) | Recommendation first, method after. | Weak evidence only (search results, not their pages). Not relied on. |
| The Markup's Blacklight | Could not confirm the result layout from the page I could load. Not relied on. | none |

### 1.2 What makes a page read as "generic SaaS", checked against the current site

Sources: [Overpass: why SaaS sites look the same](https://www.overpass.studio/blog/why-saas-websites-look-the-same), [a critique of AI-landing-page heroes](https://uxskill.laithjunaidy.com/blog/ai-landing-page-hero-generic.html), search summaries.

| Generic marker | Current site (before this audit) |
|---|---|
| Gradient or dark glowing hero | No. Flat white / near-black. Good. |
| Centred hero, vague benefit headline ("Build. Ship. Scale.") | No. Left-aligned, and the headline is the actual question. Good. |
| Three feature cards | The **/tools/ page was a 3-up card grid** (visually close to it). Now replaced by a ledger. |
| Rounded-lg everywhere | Mostly sharp corners. One remaining rounded shape: the tool-page reason callout (`border-radius: 0.4rem`). Fixed. |
| Stock or 3D illustration, tilted product screenshot | None. Good. |
| Big soft-shadow cards, pill badges, purple accent | None: keylines, sharp badges, navy accent. Good. |
| Inter / system-ui default font | Arial and Helvetica. Plain, but the intended condensed face **did not render at all** (bug 2.1 #1), so the site was Arial 700 headings on every page. |
| "Browse / Get started" CTA button as the only hero action | The hero's only control was a "Browse 33 tools" button, not the thing people came to do (look a tool up). Now a search box. |

Overall verdict: the Compliance Label direction is genuinely distinctive and worth keeping. It was being undermined by implementation bugs (headings not styled, page-level links in browser blue, no vertical padding on `main`) rather than by the concept.

### 2.2 Things checked that were fine (or had no problem)

- **Horizontal overflow:** none at 375, 768 or 1280 in light or dark on any of 11 pages. Measured `scrollWidth <= clientWidth` on all 66 page/size/theme combinations, before and after. Long `data_retention` and long source URLs wrap (`overflow-wrap`/`word-break` present).
- **Contrast (WCAG AA), computed:** all text tokens pass in both themes. Lowest are light-mode badge text: green `#1e7a34` on `#e6f4ea` = 4.76:1 (AA needs 4.5:1 for normal text; this text is 12 px, so it passes but with almost no margin), amber `#92600a` on `#fef3e2` = 4.91:1, red 5.55:1, caveat 6.70:1, gray 8.18:1. Body 18.6:1, muted 6.81:1. Dark: all above 6.8:1. No failure found. Full numbers are printed by `research/ui_audit_assets/tools/contrast.mjs`.
- **Lang** is `en`. **Heading hierarchy:** one h1 per page, h2s below (after fix, the label panels and the submit panel titles are h2, not `<p>`).
- **Keyboard:** tab order is skip link, brand, nav, page content in reading order, then footer. Every stop has a visible ring (after the global-style fix).
- **Reduced motion:** the site has no animation or transitions. Nothing to gate.
- **Unknown tool URL:** returns a 404. (The SEO agent has already added `404.astro`; I did not touch it.) Not tested in the built site with Cloudflare Pages, only in `astro preview`.
- **Link targets:** source links open in a new tab with `noopener`. I added `noreferrer` and a screen-reader-only "(opens in a new tab)".

### 2.3 Security: untrusted extracted text

Result: **no XSS gap found; one defence-in-depth gap fixed.**

- No `set:html`, `innerHTML`, `eval`, `define:vars` or `is:inline` anywhere in `frontend/src`. There is no client-side search index or embedded JSON. Astro escapes every interpolated string.
- Tested with a fixture build (copy of the site with one malicious tool: name `<img src=x onerror=alert(1)>Evil`, summary `</p><script>alert('sum')</script>`, retention `<svg onload=alert(2)>`, sources `javascript:alert(3)`, a `"><script>` URL, and a `data:` URL). Every hit in the built HTML was either escaped (`&lt;img...`) in text nodes, or inside a double-quoted attribute value (`content="..."`, `data-name="..."`) where `<` is inert and `"` is escaped. The fixture (in `research/ui_audit_assets/xss_fixture/`) never touched `frontend/data/tools.json`.
- **The gap:** `source_urls` were rendered as `href={url}` with no scheme check. Astro escapes the attribute text, but a `javascript:` or `data:` URL would still have been a clickable link. In the fixture, the `javascript:` and `data:` URLs no longer become links (`safeHttpUrl()` in `src/verdicts.ts` only allows `http:` and `https:`; anything else renders as plain text). Today every real source URL is https, so this was not exploitable now.
- Client-side scripts I added or changed (`/tools/` filter, SubmitTool) only use `textContent`, `dataset` and DOM show/hide/reorder of existing nodes. Tested in the browser: a search for `<img src=x onerror=alert(1)>` shows as literal text and injects no elements.

## 4. Left as proposals (not built)

Mock: `research/ui_mocks/tool_page_options.html` (self-contained, light and dark, fonts in `research/ui_mocks/fonts/`, all OFL).

### P1. Tool-page layout: make the verdict the biggest thing on the page (recommend Option A)

| Option | What it is | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Verdict slab** (recommended) | The verdict becomes the largest type in the label panel (like Calories on the FDA label), a double rule marks "qualified", the tool's own words are labelled "From their policy", and unrated is a hatched "We don't know" block. | Web font (about 90 KB self-hosted) + a template change. | Keeps everything already chosen. |
| B. Register entry | Serif headline, verdict as a stamp, the policy's words as an attributed pull quote, hairline fact rows. | Two web fonts, bigger visual change. | Most distinctive, but leaves the "label" idea. |
| C. Keep today's layout | What I built now (badge beside name + prominent reason + label panel + meaning line). | Free. | Fine if you don't want another round. |

Caveat on A2: replacing the word "Unrated" with "We don't know" changes the badge vocabulary defined in the charter. I would keep "Unrated" as the label and use the hatching for the block. Your call.

### P2. Typography: self-host a condensed face
"Helvetica Neue Condensed, Arial Narrow" is a system-font gamble: it works on Apple and Windows/Office, not on Android or most Linux. Barlow Condensed (OFL) self-hosted removes the variance and avoids any Google Fonts request, which suits a privacy product. Specimen is at the top of the mock. Building it needs your OK on the palette of fonts. **Recommendation: yes**, but only together with Option A so the face is doing visible work.

### P3. Badge text case and length
GOV.UK research says uppercase is harder to read for longer text, and the company-wide badge is 40+ characters (wraps to two lines in the list). Options: keep uppercase (current, on-direction), or sentence case for that one badge only, or shorten the label to "Company-wide policy" and let the reason carry the rest. **Recommendation: shorten to "Company-wide policy"** (the filter chip and register already use that). Needs your decision because badge copy is verdict vocabulary.

### P4. Show summaries as quotes
Summaries are often verbatim policy fragments, but some are ours ("Policy text is shared across…"). Nothing in `tools.json` says which is which, so I could not add quote marks and an attribution safely. A `summary_is_quote` flag (backend/data change) would allow the "From their policy" label in Option A to be honest.

### P5. Theme toggle
There is no toggle; the site follows the OS. That is fine for the ICP. If you want one, it is small (the CSS already supports `data-theme`). **Recommendation: skip.**

### P6. Cross-check on "Reviewed by a human"
The panel row shows "No — auto-published" for 16 of 33 tools. That is accurate and honest, but it is prominent. Optional: only show the row when it is "Yes"? I'd keep it; it is a trust cue. Not changed.

### Product gap worth knowing (not a UI bug)
The site is static and reads `tools.json` at build time. A check submitted through the form that auto-publishes will not appear in the site's Tools list until the site is rebuilt and redeployed. The success message now says "New results can take a while to show up in the list on this site" instead of promising it will be published right away. If instant appearance matters, that needs the site to read from the API or a scheduled rebuild.

Tool page: design options (mock only, nothing here is built on the site)

The live site currently uses the "Compliance Label" direction with small badges beside the tool name. Two ways to push it further are below, plus a type comparison. Both are drawn with a real tool's data (ChatGPT, caveats; Zoom AI Companion, unrated). Try dark mode: both follow prefers-color-scheme.

0. Type: why the headline face is not reliably condensed today

The site asks for "Helvetica Neue Condensed", then "Arial Narrow". iOS and macOS/Windows with Office have Arial Narrow; Android and most Linux browsers do not, so many visitors get plain Arial and the "condensed regulatory label" look disappears. A self-hosted Barlow Condensed (OFL, about 22 KB per weight, no third-party request) makes it the same for everyone. Left: current stack on this machine. Right: Barlow Condensed.

Option A: "Verdict slab" (label-forward)

The verdict becomes the biggest type on the page, the way the FDA's 2016 Nutrition Facts redesign made Calories the largest type on the label. Today the verdict is a small badge next to the name. Uncertainty gets its own pattern: hatching (as maps use for "no data") instead of a mild colour, so "unrated" cannot be read as "a bit okay". Keeps the black keylines, tabular mono numerals, sharp corners. Adds a webfont.

Option B: "Register entry" (editorial / data-journalism)

Reads like an entry in a public register or a newspaper fact box rather than a product card. A serif headline and the tool's own words set as a pull quote with a source line (data-journalism practice: every claim carries its source and date). The verdict is a rubber-stamp tag. Least like a SaaS page; less "regulatory label" than today, so it moves away from the current direction. Adds two webfonts (Source Serif 4 and Barlow).

Recommendation

Pick A. It keeps everything Elliot already chose (keylines, condensed head, mono numerals, sharp corners), fixes the biggest hierarchy problem (verdict is a small badge), gives "we don't know" a visual language that cannot be misread, and the webfont makes the direction look the same on every device. B is more distinctive but abandons the label idea and the two-font change is bigger. A also costs about 90 KB of self-hosted fonts total (three Barlow Condensed weights, two Barlow, one Plex Mono weight). If you want to try B on one page first, use the tool page only and keep the list as it is.

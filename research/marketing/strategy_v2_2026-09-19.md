## 1. The strategy on one page

**Who.** Someone about to sign up for, or already using, an AI tool who wonders whether it trains on what they type. Not a privacy expert, not buying for a company. They ask in a search box or a community thread, usually about a named tool.

**The promise.** "See what an AI tool's own privacy policy says about training on your data, before you sign up. Every badge links to the words it came from; when the policy is unclear, we say Unrated." (v1 message A as headline, B and C as proof.) Always alongside: it describes what the policy says on the date checked, it is not legal advice, and business and consumer plans can differ.

**Why now.** People already ask, and the answers they find are articles and trackers ([Sonomos](https://sonomos.ai/blog/which-ai-tools-train-on-your-data-2026/), [Voibe](https://www.getvoibe.com/resources/ai-privacy-tracker/)). Terms also move: GitHub changed Copilot's defaults in April (above). That favours a checker that shows the answer where you are and links the clause. Honest limit: 33 tools (32 indexable), zero users, no store listing. "Why now" is a reason to be findable, not evidence of demand.

**The three things that will move the needle (3 to 5 hours a week means three).**
1. **Make the tool pages findable and trustworthy.** They are the one asset that works while Elliot sleeps. Fix trust gaps first (privacy page, contact route, last-checked date, tier line, title wording), read the Search Console exports, then add the 22 researched-but-unsubmitted tools after a spot-check. Each verified tool is another page that can answer one real question.
2. **Get the extension into the stores, Firefox and Chrome in the same week.** The badge where you browse is what competitors do differently, and store search is the second discovery surface. Blocked by the deploy, a real store icon, the published privacy page and screenshots (`research/extension_store_listing_2026-09-19.md`).
3. **One honest public moment, then useful answers.** One Show HN in Elliot's own words, one data piece from the catalog ("what 33 privacy policies say about training, and how many were unclear"), and a few genuine replies where people already ask the question. No campaign.

**Deliberately not doing.**
- Anything that spends money: ads, sponsored posts, paid tools.
- Product Hunt (installing an extension hurts conversion; revisit once listed), press or creator pitching (contacting people is Elliot's call, and premature), a blog treadmill, several social accounts.
- Organisation or vendor messaging; announcing premium (unbuilt).
- On-site analytics or pixels. Search Console plus store dashboards suffice for 30 days, and site analytics would contradict the draft privacy policy ("no analytics").
- Near-duplicate pages, "X vs Y" pages, and the words "safe", "unsafe", "certified", "human reviewed", "complete".
- Publicising any verdict I think is wrong or stale (the two above).

**Firm proposals (v1's open items).**
- Premium: say nothing until it exists. Voice: plain and warm on every public page, About included.
- Stores: Firefox (free) and Chrome ($5 per the store pack) in week 2, after deploy and privacy page. Show HN does not wait for approval; the website lookup needs no install, which is what Show HN asks for ([Show HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html)).
- Tiers: add a fixed line to every tool page now ("business and enterprise plans often have different terms"), per-tool notes later for split tools (DeepL, GitHub Copilot, Gamma; `research/tool_list.md`).
- Name: keep PolicyPeek for now. Neighbours are PolicyPak, PolicyPeak (GitHub) and Firefox's Peek-a-Policy (v1, store pack). Watch Search Console for "policypeak" queries.
- Measurement: Search Console and store dashboards only.

**Decisions for Elliot (recommendation after the arrow).**
1. Rule on Granola, and re-check GitHub Copilot, before anything links to them? -> Yes, before launch.
2. Drop "GDPR Check" from tool-page titles now, and rename badge labels ("Compliant", "Not compliant") to wording that describes the policy, before Show HN? -> Drop the title text now, decide labels this week; HN readers will push on "compliant".
3. Post under your own name and be online for the Show HN day? -> Yes; the disclosure is the trust.
4. Add contact email, privacy page, a "who built this" line on About and a last-checked date on tool pages before any public post? -> Yes, all four.
5. Run the 22-tool batch before or after launch? -> After a 5 to 8 tool spot-check, and before Show HN if possible (catalog reads ~55, not 33).

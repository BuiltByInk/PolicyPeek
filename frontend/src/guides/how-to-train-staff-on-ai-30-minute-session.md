# How to Train Staff on AI: A 30-Minute Session for Small Teams

*Last checked: 24 October 2026. Based on the European Commission's AI literacy Q&A, ICO guidance and AI tools' own published policies. Not legal advice.*

You don't need a course provider to train a small team on AI. A 30-minute session covering your approved tools, what never goes in, how to check output and who to tell when something goes wrong covers the basics. Keep a record of who attended. Below is a ready-to-run agenda.

## Is AI training required?

If the EU AI Act reaches your business, yes in some form. Article 4 requires businesses that use AI systems to take measures to support their staff's AI literacy. The European Commission says there's no one-size-fits-all format and no certificate is needed, but relying only on a tool's instructions is often not enough, and an internal record of training is enough as evidence. ([European Commission AI literacy Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers)) For whether it applies to you, see [EU AI Act Article 4 for small UK businesses](https://getpolicypeek.com/guides/eu-ai-act-article-4-ai-literacy-small-uk-business/).

Even where it doesn't, UK GDPR applies whenever staff put personal data into AI tools, so the rules need to reach the people using them. ([ICO advice for small organisations](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/getting-started-with-gdpr/data-protection-advice-on-common-topics))

## The 30-minute agenda

**0–5 min: Why we're doing this.** AI tools are useful, and they also send what you type to another company. Some of those companies train on it. PolicyPeek's checks of 59 AI tools (24 October 2026) found 14 that train on your content with no opt-out found. ([PolicyPeek](https://getpolicypeek.com/tools/))

**5–10 min: Our approved tools.** Show the list from your AI policy: which tools, which plan, which account. If you don't have a policy yet, see [Do UK small businesses need an AI policy?](https://getpolicypeek.com/guides/do-uk-small-businesses-need-an-ai-policy/)

**10–15 min: What never goes in.** Customer and staff personal data, health information, passwords, unreleased financials, anything under an NDA. Show one real example of a safe rewrite, such as "a customer" instead of a name.

**15–20 min: Hallucination demo.** The Commission's Q&A says staff should be told about specific risks such as hallucination. ([European Commission AI literacy Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers)) Ask an AI tool something about your own business or industry it's likely to get wrong, and check the answer together. The rule: a person checks anything that goes to a customer.

**20–25 min: Settings walk-through.** Open the tool your team uses most and find its training or "improve the model" setting together. Check each tool's policy on [PolicyPeek](https://getpolicypeek.com/).

**25–30 min: Mistakes and questions.** Who to tell if the wrong data goes in, and that telling quickly matters more than blame.

## Keep a training record

Write down the date, who attended, who ran it and what was covered. A shared spreadsheet is fine. Repeat a shorter version when you adopt a new tool or hire someone new.

## Tailor it by role

The Commission says training should take account of people's technical knowledge, experience and the context the AI is used in. ([European Commission AI literacy Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers)) In practice: anyone using AI for customer emails needs the data rules most; anyone using it for code or content needs the checking rules most.

## FAQ

### How long should AI training for staff be?

For a small team using everyday tools like ChatGPT, a 30-minute session plus a short refresher when tools change is a practical starting point. No minimum length is set by the EU AI Act.

### Do staff need an AI certificate?

No. The European Commission says no certificate is needed; an internal record of training and guidance is enough.

### What should AI training for staff cover?

Approved tools, what data must never be entered, checking AI output for mistakes, training settings, and who to tell when something goes wrong.

### How often should we repeat AI training?

When you adopt a new tool, when a tool changes its terms, and when someone new joins. Many teams also do a short yearly refresher.
